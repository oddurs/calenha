# decisions

A running log. Newest at the bottom. Each entry: what was decided, and why.

### D-001 · the language is data; the code is a compiler
All language content lives in TOML under `lang/`. The Python toolkit reads it,
validates it and renders it. Like a design system: tokens are the source of truth;
everything else is generated.

### D-002 · python, zero runtime dependencies
Python 3.11+ for `tomllib` in the standard library. Quick to change while the
language is still finding itself. Dev tools (pytest, ruff) via `uv`.

### D-003 · stress rests on the second-to-last syllable
The Portuguese music. Two exceptions, both audible in the spelling:
a word ending in a nasal (*mirão*, *nevoã*) draws stress to its end, and an acute
accent marks a long vowel, which always takes the stress (*thalírë*).

### D-004 · c is always /k/
As in Quenya. There is no soft *c* and no letter *k* in the modern spelling; *k*
survives only in old forms.

### D-005 · final e is written ë
Borrowed from Tolkien. It reminds the reader the vowel is sounded, and it gives
word-endings a little lift on the page. A plain *e* may not end a word, and *ë* may
appear nowhere else.

### D-006 · digraphs ch, lh, nh, th
*ch* /ʃ/, *lh* /ʎ/, *nh* /ɲ/ from Portuguese; *th* /θ/ from English. *lh* and *nh*
never begin a word, as in Portuguese, which keeps them soft and medial.

### D-007 · no imperative mood
The plain verb is an invitation. See `context/principles.md` §6.

### D-008 · tense is a tide
Past and future are marked by the particles *vaza* (ebb) and *chea* (flood);
the present, slack water, is unmarked. From Portuguese *vazante* and *cheia*.

### D-009 · the breath mark ·
A middle dot. Between words it is a pause that closes the scope of a particle
(the language's quiet nod to a block ending). Inside a word it breaks the word open
with longing: *mi·rão*, "a memory that hurts to hold".

### D-010 · the land is Solmara, the people the Calenhi
*Solmara*, sun and sea. *Calenhi*, the calm ones, formed from *calenha* by the
people-suffix *-i*.

### D-011 · old Calenha, and the order of its changes
The language has a history: an older, harder speech (k, doubled vowels, plain
endings) worn smooth over generations. Changes run in order, as real sound change
does. The long vowels formed *before* n and l softened — otherwise *\*thaliire*
would have become *thalhirë*. Order is part of the story; `calenha check` holds every
old form to it.

### D-012 · taste is data
The generator's ear lives in `[euphony]` in `lang/phonology.toml`: tiers of sounds
(flow, breath, hush, spice) and how welcome each is. It rewards softness, open
syllables, varied vowels and an unhurried rhythm, and penalises stammers and
colliding vowels. It only proposes. A person reads the word aloud and chooses.

### D-013 · no noun may end in a u-glide
A word ending in *-au*, *-eu* or *-ou* looks like a verb's invitation. Nouns,
names and qualities avoid it, as they avoid a plain final *-u*.

### D-014 · the dictionary page (superseded in part by D-015)
One quiet column on fog-grey, set in Gentium Book Plus (made for linguists; it
carries IPA properly). The only colour that speaks is poppy gold, under each
stressed syllable — so the page teaches the language's music as it is read. The
opening line breathes in word by word, once.

### D-015 · the website
The single dictionary page grew into a static site, generated by `calenha build` with
no dependencies: plain HTML, one stylesheet, links with explicit `.html` so it works
anywhere. Every word has its own page — how to say it, what it is made from, how it
changed from Old Calenha, its family, and where it is used. Calenha words in example
lines link to their pages. The look of D-014 carries through every page.

### D-016 · the unspoken
The site keeps a page of words the generator has found but no one has given a
meaning. They change as the lexicon grows (the seed is the size of the lexicon). It
is an invitation, in keeping with the language: here are sounds waiting for a sense.
