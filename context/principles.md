# principles

These are the rules behind the rules. When a decision is unclear, come back here.

## 1. sound comes first

A word that sounds ugly is wrong, however logical its derivation. Read every new word
aloud before accepting it. Prefer liquids (*l, r*), nasals (*m, n, nh*), soft
fricatives (*s, v, th*) and open syllables. Stops are spice, not the meal.

## 2. it should look as good as it sounds

Calenha is written as much as it is spoken. Diacritics are used sparingly and always
mean something: the acute marks a long, held vowel; the tilde marks nasality; the
diaeresis marks a final, sounded *ë*. A line of Calenha on a page should look calm —
lowercase, airy, with room around it.

## 3. the wildness must carry meaning

The E.E. Cummings influence is real, but nothing is decoration. Lowercase is the
default, so a **Capital** is a bow. A space is a pause the reader must honour. A word
broken by a breath mark (*mi·rão*) aches. If a typographic liberty doesn't change
meaning, it doesn't belong in the grammar.

## 4. code is a seasoning

The programming influence should be felt, not seen. Particles behave like keywords,
conditions have scope, and some forms loop. But a reader who has never written code
should simply find the language elegant. Never introduce brackets, operators, or
anything that looks like syntax for its own sake.

## 5. grow from roots

New words come from roots and affixes before they come from nothing. A dictionary
grown this way feels like it belongs to one people. When a word is coined from
nothing, give it a root so it can have children.

## 6. invite, never command

There is no imperative mood. A verb in its plain form is an invitation: *velu* is
"come wander", never "go". This is the moral centre of the language and should not be
worked around.

## 7. the data is the truth

Everything about the language lives in `lang/`. Generated files (`dist/`) are never
edited by hand. If the toolkit disagrees with the data, fix the rule or fix the word —
and record the decision in `context/decisions.md`.

## inspirations

Taken freely and openly:

- **Quenya and Sindarin** — the feeling of an old, lived-in tongue; the final *ë*,
  the acute for length, the habit of compound names.
- **Portuguese** — the nasal *ão* and *ã*, the soft *nh* and *lh*, the music of
  penultimate stress, many roots (*névoa*, *ouro*, *maré*, *vazante*, *cheia*).
- **Spanish place names of California** — the bones of the landscape's names.
- **English** — a touch of *th* and *w*, for crispness and air.
- **E.E. Cummings** — lowercase, space as meaning, words that come apart.
- **programming languages** — keywords, scope, loops, named arguments — quietly.
