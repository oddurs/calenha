# the world

Calenha is spoken in **Solmara**, a California that sits one step to the side of ours.
The coastline is the same, and the light is the same, but nothing was ever paved.
The land was never hurried, so neither were the people.

## the land

Solmara is read from the sea inward, the way the fog travels.

- **the Algamar** — the kelp forests. A woodland under the water, swaying, gold-green.
  The Calenhi think of it as the first forest, and the trees on land as its echo.
- **the Nevoã coast** — where the marine layer rolls in every morning and burns off by noon.
  Fog is not gloom here. It is a gentle visitor, and it is greeted.
- **the Miraluma groves** — the redwoods. The Calenhi believe the oldest trees hold
  memory, which is why the word for redwood is *memory standing in light*.
- **the Ouressa hills** — oak savanna and grass that turns gold in summer, with poppy
  fields in spring.
- **Pedralta** — the high granite to the east, snow on its shoulders, where the
  condors (*altavel*, the high wanderers) circle.
- **Lonjura** — the desert beyond the mountains. *The far place.* Quiet, wide, and
  once in a long while it blooms all at once.

## the people

The speakers call themselves the **Calenhi**, *the calm ones*, and their language
**Calenha**, *the calm speech*.

They are not passive. They are patient. The difference is attention: a Calenhi can
wait a whole morning for the fog to lift because they are watching it lift.

What they value, and therefore what the language has many words for:

- light, in all its kinds and hours
- water that moves — tide, river, wave, fog
- memory, especially memory held by places and trees
- wandering without a destination
- invitation, welcome, the open hand

What they value less, and therefore what the language has few words for:

- hurry
- ownership
- commands

## time

The Calenhi do not count hours. They count **tides** for the shape of a day and
**seasons** for the shape of a year.

| season | name | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| spring | *ouressa* | the poppies open, the hills go gold |
| early summer | *nevoã* | the fog season, soft mornings |
| autumn | *brisalma* | the hot dry wind from the desert; respected, a little feared |
| winter | *chuvenha* | the rain season — *the speech of rain* |

Within a day, time is a tide: something **ebbing** has passed, something **flooding**
is coming, and **slack water** is now. The grammar carries this directly
(see `docs/grammar.md`).

## the feeling to protect

If you read a line of Calenha aloud, it should feel like late afternoon on a
headland: warm stone, cool air, the sea doing its slow work below. Every decision in
this project should be checked against that feeling.
